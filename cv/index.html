Mobile app · 2024 - 2025

HayWay

HayWay is a taxi booking app created to improve the daily ride experience: a simple, fast and reliable way to order a taxi. This case study walks through the whole UX process, from research to design direction and the final UI.

RoleUX Research · UX/UI Design
PlatformiOS
Duration6 months
Year2024 - 2025
Full case on Behance
Overview

A simple, fast and reliable way to order a taxi

Problems
  • Users struggle with confusing map navigation and wrong pick-up points.
  • Payment options are limited or unclear.
  • There is little trust in drivers, with no clear rating or review system.
Solutions
  • User interviews and usability testing to explore the needs in depth.
  • Intuitive navigation that gets the pick-up point right.
  • More flexible payments, plus driver ratings and ride reviews that build trust.
Process

Double Diamond, four stages

01Discover
  • Quantitative research
  • Qualitative research
  • Competitive analysis
  • Hypothesis
02Define
  • Affinity mapping
  • User persona
  • Empathy map

  • Card sorting
  • Feature prioritisation
03Develop
  • Information architecture
  • User flows
  • High-fidelity wireframes
  • Prototypes
  • Prototype testing, first iteration
04Deliver
  • Design system
  • UI design

  • Learnings and takeaways
  • Future plans
Research

What the research showed

30

people answered the survey about taxi habits, payments and priorities

10

in-depth interviews about booking, payments, ride history and feedback

3

local taxi apps compared: GG Taxi, Yandex Taxi and Eco Taxi

53%

of respondents prefer a dark mode in a taxi app

  1. 01

    Pick-up accuracy is the biggest frustration: wrong street names, the wrong side of the street and inaccurate map positions make the start of a ride stressful.

  2. 02

    Limited flexibility hurts: people cannot book a taxi in advance or change the payment method during a trip.

  3. 03

    Useful features stay hidden: several users did not know ride history existed, so they missed tools for lost items and expenses.

  4. 04

    Group rides are not supported: there is no way to split the fare between friends.

  5. 05

    Feedback is polarised: most people rate only very good or very bad trips, and few leave written reviews.

HypothesisBy simplifying booking, streamlining group payments and making ride history and support easier to reach, users will be more focused and satisfied, and HayWay will earn lasting trust.
Persona
Anahit KarapetyanSoftware Engineer

Takes a taxi to work and back home every day, and travels around Armenia with friends in her free time.

Needs
  • Book a taxi for an out-of-town trip that arrives at the right time
  • Split the fare with friends
  • Request a taxi to the exact pick-up point
  • Tell her bank cards apart in the app
Problems
  • Forgets which card is selected and gets confused
  • Cannot switch between card and cash during a trip
  • The driver may not have change
  • Struggles to order a taxi to the correct side of the street
Solution
Booking rides for future

Choose a date and time and the taxi arrives when it is needed, for airport runs and trips out of town.

Taxi with Friends

Share a link, friends join the ride and the total is split between everyone in the group.

Side of the street

Pick the left or right side of the street for the pick-up, the problem riders mentioned most.

Cards you can tell apart

Rename cards, set one as primary and switch the payment method without confusion.

Testing
Prototype testing · first iteration10 participants from different countries, 4 tasks
  • Every task was completed by all participants.
  • 6 of 10 forgot to choose the side of the street at the start and noticed only later.
  • 7 of 10 looked for saved addresses in the search bar instead of the profile.
User testing · second iteration11 participants, 5 tasks on the final UI
  • All five tasks were completed, most within the ideal or average time.
  • Animations in “Taxi with Friends” slowed people down, so I removed them.
  • “Set as primary” was made more visible after users missed it.
  • Many expected payments and addresses under Settings, so navigation labels were reworked.

Takeaways
What I learned
  • Listening to users pays off: interviews and observation showed things I would not have found alone.
  • Keep it simple: a useful feature does not help if people cannot find it.
  • Test early and often to catch problems before building the wrong thing.
  • Small details like icon placement and button labels change how easy the app feels.
Future plans
  • Make scheduling more visible for group trips and rides abroad.
  • Child safety options, like requesting a child seat.
  • A monthly report of rides, spending and frequent destinations.
  • Personal options such as quiet rides, music and accessibility settings.

Skatey

Skatey is an online shop for all things ice skating, for experts and beginners alike. It offers high-quality skates and accessories, a user-friendly interface and everything from laces to skates.

RoleUX Research · UI/UX Design
PlatformDesktop · Mobile web
Duration6 months
Year2024
Full case on Behance
Overview

Ice skates, easy to choose for anyone

Problems
  • Users had trouble finding product reviews.
  • Filtering and sorting menus in most online stores feel complicated.
  • Both make people spend much longer on a site, which they find frustrating.
Solutions
  • User interviews and research to understand how to solve these problems.
  • A store structure and filters built around what skaters actually look for.
  • A better website for beginners and pros at the same time.
Process

Double Diamond, four stages

01Discover
  • Quantitative analysis
  • Qualitative analysis
  • Competitive analysis
  • Hypothesis
02Define
  • User persona
  • Customer journey map
  • Affinity mapping
  • Feature prioritisation
03Develop
  • Information architecture
  • User flows (3)
  • Wireframes
  • Prototypes
  • Design system
04Deliver
  • High-fidelity prototype
  • UI design
  • User testing
  • Learnings and takeaways
  • Future plans
Research

What the research showed

9

people who bought skates online took the survey

9

interviews about how people choose and buy skates

4

stores compared: PowerSlide, Skates.co, SkateHut and HockeyPoint

78%

name price, quality and size as what matters most

  1. 01

    The most common problems are sorting and search. People want only the essentials on a product: price, colour, size and brand.

  2. 02

    CTA buttons should be distinctive and stand out from the background.

  3. 03

    Opinions on reviews were split, so I followed the convention of large stores and placed them at the bottom of the page.

  4. 04

    Every participant prefers leather skates, and some want eco-leather, so leather comes first with its own eco-leather section.

  5. 05

    People replace skates only when the old pair is worn out.

Persona
Tom Petrosyan, 27Fitness Trainer

Wants to try ice skating as a way to relax after work. He prefers owning his equipment, so he plans to buy his own pair of skates.

Needs
  • Up-to-date information about skates
  • The best price across all options
  • The right product in as few steps as possible
  • Reviews he can trust
Problems
  • CTA buttons that do not stand out
  • An interface that is not user-friendly
  • Reviews placed where he cannot find them
  • Outdated information and too few filters
Solution
Filters that make sense

Gender, type, material, size, colour and brand, with “Clear” and “Apply”, also after a search.

A clear catalogue

Skates, Protection and Blades, each with New arrivals, Best sellers, Women, Men, Kids, Type and Brand.

Distinctive CTAs

“Buy now” and “Add to cart” stand out on every card, with the discount and old price visible.

Desktop and mobile

A bold storefront with the Winter 2024 collection, and a mobile catalogue with filter chips.

Testing
User testing3 participants on the final design
  • All 3 called the website “user friendly”.
  • Everyone said it is easy to navigate and the filters are understandable.
  • On average the flow by brand took 4 minutes and the flow by skate type 3 minutes.

Meduza

Instant updates, breaking news. A redesign concept for the Meduza app and website that brings news, podcasts, audiobooks, stories and books into one calm interface that is easy to read on the phone and on the web.

RoleUX/UI redesign
PlatformMobile app · Website
TypeConcept
YearJanuary 2024
Full case on Behance
Overview

All of Meduza in one calm place

Meduza publishes much more than news. Podcasts, audiobooks, stories and books all compete for the same screen, and a busy feed makes it hard to see what matters today.

The goal of the redesign was to give every type of content a clear home, make the news quick to scan, and keep the app and the website consistent with each other.

Problems
  • Content types are scattered, so readers do not know where to look for podcasts or books.
  • A dense feed makes it hard to tell the important news from the rest.
  • The app and the website feel like two different products.
Solutions
  • One top navigation with a section for each content type.
  • A home built from two clear blocks: “News today” and “Blitz news”.
  • A calm dark interface with one amber accent and shared components on mobile and web.
Process

From audit to a calm interface

01Audit
  • Reviewed the current app and website
  • Listed every content type and where it lives
  • Marked where reading is slowed down by clutter
02Structure
  • Navigation: Audiobooks, About Meduza, News, Podcasts, Stories, Books
  • Home built from “News today” and “Blitz news”
  • Mobile tab bar: Home, Favorite, Saved, Settings
03Design
  • Dark theme with one amber accent
  • News cards with image, summary, time and bookmark
  • Podcast cards with episode count and “Listen”
04Deliver
  • Website home and news pages
  • Mobile app screens
  • Shared components for both platforms
Highlights
News today

Large cards with an image, a headline, a short summary, the publish time and a bookmark, so the day’s main stories are clear at a glance.

Blitz news

Short news for readers who want the headlines in a minute, separated from long reads.

Podcasts in one library

Covers, the number of episodes and a quick “Listen” button on mobile, without leaving the app.

A simple tab bar

Home, Favorite, Saved and Settings keep the mobile app easy to move around with one thumb.

Visual style
Key screens
  1. 01Web home
  2. 02News today
  3. 03Blitz news
  4. 04Podcasts on mobile

Takeaways
What I took from it
  • A single accent colour is enough when the hierarchy is strong.
  • Splitting quick news from long reads makes the home much easier to scan.
  • Designing mobile and web together keeps components and decisions consistent.
Ideas for next steps
  • Screens for audiobooks, stories and books.
  • A light theme for daytime reading.
  • Personal sections based on saved and favourite content.

Henaket

At the Informational Systems Agency of Armenia I work on Henaket, the design system for Armenian government services: the components, templates and rules that public digital services across the country are built on.

RoleUI/UX Designer
FocusHenaket design system
ToolFigma
SinceMarch 2026
Henaket design system in Figma
Overview

Henaket, one system for every government service

Henaket is the official design system for Armenian government services. It gives every team the same foundations, components and templates, so services used across the country look and work the same way.

My work is to grow the system, keep it healthy and check that real products follow it.

Challenges
  • Many teams build many services, so look and behaviour drift apart.
  • Components have to work for every citizen, in every kind of service.
  • Live websites do not always follow the system.
What I do about it
  • New Figma components and templates that teams can build on.
  • Fixes and improvements to existing components.
  • Manual audits that show exactly where a site breaks from the system.
Scope

  • Fixes to existing components
  • Ready for product teams to reuse

03Updates
  • Contributed to two major Henaket updates
  • Improvements based on what teams need
04Audits
  • 30+ manual website audits
  • Almost all of them government sites
  • Design-system compliance and quality

Bizzy

Bizzy is a digital marketing agency that builds identities and marketing strategies for brands. I designed its full website and the complete admin panel, both on one shared component system.

RoleUI/UX Designer
PlatformWeb · Admin panel
ScopeWebsite · Admin · Components
SinceJuly 2025
Overview

Data-driven marketing, creative by design

An agency site has one job: show visitors who the agency is and what it has done, then lead them to a conversation. Behind it, the team needs an admin panel to manage projects, services and incoming data.

Goals
  • Visitors should reach proof of work and the offer in one or two clicks.
  • The brand has to feel bold and recognisable on every page.
  • The admin panel handles a lot of data and must stay usable on smaller screens.
Approach
  • A hero with two clear paths: “View Projects” and “Our Services”.
  • A deep purple base with pink and yellow accents and the bee mark.
  • One component system shared by the website and the admin, with dense but readable views.
Process

From structure to one system

01Structure
  • Site map: Home, About Us, Projects, Services, Contact Us
  • Clear calls to action on every page
  • A conversion-minded order of sections
02Identity
  • Deep purple base
  • Pink and yellow accents
  • The bee as a recurring brand mark
03Admin panel
  • Data-dense tables and views
  • Responsive layouts
  • Flows for managing site content
04System
  • Shared buttons, cards and forms
  • One library for the site and the admin
  • Built to scale with new pages
